Televisions & Energy Use

Televisions are an essential part of many Australian households, with a wide range of brands, screen sizes, and technologies available on the market. This website explores television energy consumption data in Australia through a series of visualisations, helping users understand key trends and factors that influence energy efficiency.

4. Which type of screen technology consumes the least amount of power?

Answer: Based on the visualisation, LCD technology consumes the least amount of power, with a median average power consumption of approximately 71.35 W. In comparison, LCD (LED) screens consume about 106 W, while OLED screens have the highest median power consumption at 112.70 W.

5. What is the relationship between screen size and power use?

Answer: Based on the scatter plot, there is a positive relationship between screen size and power consumption. As the screen size increases, the average power usage generally increases as well.
Smaller televisions (around 20–40 inches) tend to consume relatively low amounts of power, whereas larger televisions (60 inches and above) show substantially higher power consumption. The highest power usage values are observed among the largest screen sizes, particularly those above 80 inches.
In conclusion, there is a moderate to strong positive correlation between screen size and power use. Larger TVs generally require more power to operate, indicating that power consumption increases as screen size increases.

6. What is the relationship between star rating and screen size?

Answer: Based on the scatter plot, there does not appear to be a strong relationship between screen size and star rating. For most screen sizes, the star ratings are spread across a wide range, typically between 3 and 7 stars.
Both small and large TVs can have either high or low star ratings, and there is no clear upward or downward trend as screen size increases. While some larger TVs achieve high star ratings, similar ratings are also found among smaller and medium-sized TVs.
In conclusion, the scatter plot suggests little to no significant correlation between screen size and star rating. Screen size alone does not appear to determine a TV's star rating, indicating that other factors such as energy efficiency, technology, and design may have a greater influence on the rating.

7. Are there differences in power consumption between brands?

Answer: Based on the box plot and bar chart, there are clear differences in power consumption between TV brands. The average power consumption varies considerably across brands, with Spark Electronics recording the highest average power usage at approximately 233.45 W, followed by Emete (182.03 W) and Samsung Electronics (155.04 W). Other brands such as Sony, Dreame, TCL, and Samsung have average power consumption values ranging from about 142 W to 153 W, while Kogan shows one of the lower averages at approximately 130.03 W. The box plot also indicates substantial variation in power consumption among models within the same brand. Overall, the results suggest that power consumption differs significantly between brands, with some brands generally producing more energy-intensive televisions than others.
